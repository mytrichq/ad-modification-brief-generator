# Writing variations that get approved

## The viewer

People scroll a feed with the sound off. Within two seconds they decide whether to stay. So:

- **The first frame and the on-screen text must work without sound.** The first spoken line supports them; it can't carry the opening alone.
- **One concrete image beats a clever line.** "One sock out, its pair comes with it" works because you can see it.
- **Real details over adjectives.** A time (7:40am), a place (the dryer), a family member. No ad-speak ("game-changer", "say goodbye to", "revolutionary").
- **Keep it short.** On-screen text up to 8 words; the first spoken line up to 14 words (about 2.5 words per second).

## The product

- State only what `data/brand.json` lists: the magnet in each cuff, click before the wash, out of the washer as a pair, 5 pairs for $29, a new delivery every quarter, sizes picked in the quiz.
- Every ending sends people to the quiz.
- Things that look harmless but are claims: a quiz length ("30-sec quiz"), "in ten seconds", "never lose a sock again", "forever", where to buy ("on their site"), free shipping, discounts. Leave them out.

## The plan for the designer

Every variation is a production plan, not just copy:

- **Footage per scene:** `reuse` (the original shot), `trim` (shortened, re-timed or cropped), `new` (needs a shot). Prefer reuse: a hook cut from the demo footage ships today.
- **Direction for the whole ad:** talent, location, voice (who, tone, pace), music, captions. Write "same as original" where nothing changes, so the designer knows what to keep. Be specific when something changes: "a dad, 35–45, who does the laundry, his own living room, warm and a bit sheepish".
- **New shots must be shootable by a UGC creator at home in one take.** Split screens, composites and costumes cost a production day; the team rejects them for tests.

## The test

- **One hypothesis per variation:** "If we …, then <metric> …, because …". The metric matches the type (see `modification-types.md`).
- **Hooks in one pack open on clearly different images,** not the same image with different words.
- **Warm, sincere tone.** The team rejected a deadpan, tired voice because it made the product feel like a chore.
